Keyboard Test

Test your keyboard online: Windows and Mac layouts, laptop or external, with or without a numpad; multi-key rollover and key chatter detection.

The keys you press are only processed right in your browser, never recorded or sent anywhere.

How to use

  1. Choose the operating system (Windows or Mac), the keyboard type (Laptop or External) and turn on “Has a numeric keypad” if yours has one.
  2. Press each key in turn: received keys turn green; keys that don't change may be dead.
  3. Hold several keys at once to see how many the keyboard registers; chattering keys are highlighted in yellow.

Frequently asked questions

A key doesn't light up?

Some keys are handled by the operating system (Fn, Print Screen on Windows, volume and brightness keys…) so web pages can't receive them; that doesn't mean the key is broken. Other keys that don't light up may be dead.

What is key chatter?

One press registers as two (typing “aa” instead of “a”), usually due to a dirty or worn switch. The tool flags any key re-registered within 35 ms of being released.

Which layout for a Mac keyboard on Windows (or vice versa)?

Choose by the keyboard plugged in. Keys are detected by position, so a Mac keyboard's ⌘ key lights up at the ⌘ spot even on Windows. On a Mac, the F1–F12 row defaults to brightness, volume…: hold fn and press to test.

Why hold several keys at once?

To see how many keys the keyboard registers at the same time (rollover). Office keyboards often only register 2–6 keys in some combinations; mechanical gaming keyboards usually register all of them (N-key rollover).
